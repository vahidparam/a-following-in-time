Reading the dots

Each white point is a recorded relationship between one user and one politician. Its horizontal position shows the latest estimated follow date across the collected snapshots. Its height shows political alignment, measured by dimension 0 of the account’s ideology score: positive scores are right-leaning and closer to Erdoğan; negative scores are closer to Kılıçdaroğlu. Zero is the scale’s reference point.

The two individual stories share a −1.5 to +1.5 axis. Scores beyond these limits appear at the edges. Accounts without a score occupy the separate “No score” band.

In cross-ideology passages, the plot shows only the highlighted week’s arrivals from the opposite side of zero. “Show everyone” restores all recorded relationships up to the story date. The large counter always reports the total audience.

The timeline expands into 2023, zooms into March–May during the first week of March, then into the election weeks during early May. Daily grid lines separate the two ballots. “Full timeline” restores the complete history.

Understanding the estimates

Follow times are estimates grouped into UTC days; spacing within each day spreads out overlapping dots. Ideology is estimated at account level and does not measure a user’s alignment at the time they followed. The view does not reconstruct unfollows or live follower totals. Hatching marks dates beyond the 26 May 2023 data cutoff.

Event comparisons show the average daily count in the seven days before an event and the seven days beginning on its date. Incomplete windows are not compared. Events provide context, not causal evidence; following does not establish political support or individual motives.

Sources & images

Kılıçdaroğlu’s first post, dated 27 August 2010, is reproduced in Habertürk’s archive. His account creation date is documented in a Marmara University thesis. Erdoğan’s first personally signed tweet, on 9 February 2015, is documented in contemporary reporting; his account had been active since 2009.

Public-domain portraits: Voice of America and U.S. Department of State. Archive photographs carry individual credits and licences. Original illustrations by Follow Motivations are symbolic depictions of the events.

The shared story: two clocks

In “Both,” one point represents one shared account. The calendar map places the KK estimated UTC follow day on X and the RTE day on Y. The unfolded view places the earlier day on X and the signed gap between timestamp estimates on Y, using a logarithmic distance. Research you

A following, in time is a science communication project by Vahid Khalili Param. It turns research on online political audiences into a story you can explore, question, and understand through data.

Vahid is a PhD candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at Sabancı University and a graduate research assistant in the VRL Lab.
